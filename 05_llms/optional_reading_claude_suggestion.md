# Section 5 Extension Reading: AI, Law, and Society

> **Note to author**: This is a prompt/outline to be developed into a full extension reading module. The five themes below were originally considered for Section 7 but fit better as supplementary material accompanying Section 5, where legal and societal questions are introduced.

---

## Prompt for Development

Write an accessible extension reading for law scholars, legislators, and law school students on the following five themes. Assume readers have completed the core Section 5 material and have a basic conceptual understanding of how machine learning models work (inputs, outputs, training data, classification). Do not assume any programming knowledge. Each theme should be 2–4 paragraphs with at least one concrete case study, legal ruling, or legislative example.

---

### Theme 1: Authorship and the Creativity Threshold

Who — or what — can author a creative work? Explore how AI challenges the human-authorship requirements embedded in copyright law. Discuss the creativity threshold and the role of human agency in AI-assisted creation. 

**Anchoring examples**: *Thaler v. Perlmutter* (U.S., 2023); the Copyright Office's position on AI-generated images; the "sufficient human control" standard emerging in practice.

---

### Theme 2: Training Data as a Legal Object

What rights govern the data used to train an AI model? Cover consent, provenance, opt-out regimes, and the unresolved tension between fair use and scraping at scale.

**Anchoring examples**: *Andersen v. Stability AI*; the EU AI Act's training data transparency requirements; *New York Times v. OpenAI* (ongoing).

---

### Theme 3: The "Black Box" Problem and Accountability

When an AI system makes a consequential decision — bail recommendations, contract scoring, medical triage — who bears liability when the reasoning cannot be explained? Connect to due process, product liability, and emerging explainability mandates.

**Anchoring examples**: COMPAS recidivism algorithm litigation; the EU AI Act's high-risk system requirements; U.S. agency guidance on algorithmic accountability.

---

### Theme 4: Deepfakes, Synthetic Media, and Epistemic Integrity

AI-generated evidence, synthetic testimony, voice and video manipulation — how do courts and legislatures authenticate what is real? Explore implications for evidence law and the integrity of the information environment.

**Anchoring examples**: Federal Rules of Evidence authentication challenges; state-level deepfake legislation (Texas, California); electoral integrity concerns.

---

### Theme 5: Regulatory Divergence and Legal Harmonization

Why do jurisdictions regulate AI so differently, and what problems does that create for multinational actors and cross-border enforcement? Introduce the EU's risk-based tier approach, the U.S. sector-by-sector model, and China's generative AI regulations — not to make readers experts in each, but to surface the structural tensions.

**Anchoring examples**: EU AI Act (2024); U.S. Executive Order on AI (Oct. 2023); China's Generative AI Interim Measures (2023).

---

## Suggested Format

- Each theme: 2–4 paragraphs + a "Discussion Questions" box (2–3 questions suitable for seminar)
- Tone: accessible, not condescending; assumes intelligent non-technical readers
- Length target: 1,500–2,500 words total
- Optional: a short comparative table of regulatory approaches for Theme 5
